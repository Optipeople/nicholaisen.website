## A decision that locks in years of consequences

A six-sided drilling line is one of the most consequential capex decisions a panel furniture producer makes in a decade. The line costs anywhere from EUR 800,000 for an entry-level cell to EUR 4 million for a high-throughput integrated configuration. It defines the throughput envelope, the changeover discipline, the operator skill profile and the integration work for the next eight to fifteen years.

It is also one of the most often badly modeled investment decisions we see. The vendor's standard business case tends to assume a product mix that does not match yours, a changeover discipline you do not yet have, and an OEE that the line will not achieve in your hands until at least year three.

This is not the vendors' fault. They are quoting their best-case capability, and their best-case capability is usually accurate. The translation to your specific business is your job, not theirs. We help with that translation; here is the framework.

## The four numbers that matter

A defensible six-sided drilling business case rests on four numbers, all of which need to be defended honestly.

<Callout title="The four numbers" tone="accent">
First, your real product mix over the next thirty-six months, not the average of the last twelve. Second, your achievable OEE in months one, twelve and twenty-four, not the vendor's nameplate figure. Third, the operating cost picture across labour, tooling, energy and maintenance, not just the headline cycle time. Fourth, the demand sensitivity that tells you what happens to payback if the volume forecast is off by twenty percent in either direction.
</Callout>

We have seen business cases miss on each of these. The most common miss is the second: assuming the line will run at vendor-quoted OEE from month three. In our experience, a well-installed six-sided drilling line reaches sustainable seventy-percent OEE somewhere between month nine and month eighteen, depending on the operator team's prior experience and the discipline of the changeover work. Anything faster is unusual and should be defended.

## The mix question

The six-sided drilling decision is, more than anything else, a question about your product mix. A line that is excellent for a narrow mix of standard sizes is mediocre for a broad mix of customized work, and vice versa. The vendors offer configurations across this spectrum, and the right configuration for you depends on a serious analysis of where your mix is going, not where it has been.

We typically run a thirty-six-month forward mix model with the client's commercial team before recommending a configuration. The exercise is uncomfortable because it forces decisions about which customer segments will grow and which will shrink. The discomfort is the point. A six-sided drilling line will outlast most of the assumptions, but the worst outcome is to specify for a mix that the business has already silently moved away from.

## The integration question

A six-sided drilling line is not a standalone purchase. It is an integration project. Material handling upstream, sequencing logic for changeover discipline, integration to the ERP for batch tracking, and the downstream buffer or pull discipline that absorbs the cycle. The vendor will quote the line. The integration usually adds twenty to forty percent to the total project cost and frequently determines whether the line achieves its target OEE.

A defensible business case includes the integration cost, the integration timeline and the integration risk explicitly. We have seen too many cases where the line capex is modeled carefully and the integration is treated as a small line item.

## What a good case looks like

A good business case for a six-sided drilling line is short. It states the proposed configuration, the product mix it is sized for, the achievable OEE curve over twenty-four months, the total project cost including integration, and the payback under three demand scenarios. It includes a section on what will be done with the existing drilling capacity once the new line is in production, because the existing capacity has an opportunity cost that affects the case. And it includes a section on what could go wrong, because every six-sided drilling project we have observed has had at least one significant surprise during ramp-up.

<PullQuote attribution="A finance director, after a six-sided drilling business-case rebuild">
We had been arguing about the wrong number for eight months. The right number was the integration cost we had not properly quoted, and the OEE curve we had been too optimistic about.
</PullQuote>

If your business case for six-sided drilling does not have those sections, defended honestly, the case is not yet ready to sign. The decision is too consequential to make on a thinner foundation than that.
