## Yield is the headline

In solid wood production, yield from the raw material is the dominant cost lever. A one-percent yield improvement on a hardwood line is worth more than most automation projects. We work the yield equation methodically: drying schedule, scanning and grading, primary breakdown decisions, and the offcut and remnant strategy that recovers value from the parts you would otherwise burn.

## The drying conversation

Drying is the operation we are asked about least and which causes the most downstream pain. A kiln schedule that is fifteen percent too aggressive saves a few days but drives moisture variation that costs hours of rework on every batch downstream. A schedule that is too conservative protects quality but starves the line. We have helped producers rework their drying technology, schedules and instrumentation to find the operating point that protects both throughput and yield.

## Energy in the kiln

The kilns are typically one of the largest single energy consumers on the site. For producers running multiple units, the difference between a coordinated and an uncoordinated load profile can be significant — both in peak demand charges and in thermal recovery from kiln exhaust. On comparable plants without heat recovery installed, we have seen a 30 percent thermal recovery potential waiting to be captured.

We approach kiln energy as part of the same system as the drying schedule and the production plan. Kilns, boilers and shift patterns are not separate decisions. When they are managed together — real-time load, temperature and moisture data connected to the scheduling layer — the energy cost moves alongside quality and throughput, not against them.

## Capital discipline

The capital decisions in solid wood production are heavy. A sawline, a kiln cluster or a planer-moulder line is a twenty-year commitment. We bring vendor-neutral discipline to the business case, the layout and the integration. Our job is to make sure the decision still looks right ten years in.
