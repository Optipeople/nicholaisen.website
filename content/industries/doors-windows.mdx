## Where the time goes

In door and window production, the time most frequently disappears in three places. The first is changeover between profile families on the moulder or the CNC cell. The second is the queue at the glazing or assembly station, where a small upstream variation in size can cost minutes per unit. The third is the rework loop on surface treatment, where defects detected late drive disproportionate cost.

We work line by line on each of these. Our experience with European door and window producers tells us where to look first.

## What we have built

We have specified and integrated machining cells for both timber-framed window producers and composite door manufacturers, optimized assembly lines that increased throughput by twenty to thirty percent without new equipment, and stood up production insight platforms that gave plant managers honest OEE for the first time in years. Our partnership clients in this segment typically see capacity gains in the first six months that defer the next major investment by twelve to eighteen.

## A note on seasonality

Door and window demand is more seasonal than most parts of the wood industry. The capacity planning conversation here is not just about peak load; it is about how to keep the right people engaged through the trough. We have helped clients structure their order book, their cross-training and their finished-goods buffer to make the seasonal swing less violent. It is rarely the first conversation, but it is often the most valuable one.
