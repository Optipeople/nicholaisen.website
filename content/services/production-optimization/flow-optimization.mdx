## Why flow before automation

Automating a poorly flowing line is one of the most expensive mistakes we see. The new robot just produces the same waste faster.

Get the flow right first. Then decide whether the cell still needs a robot, and which station should get one. The answer is often not the station you started with.

## What we have learned

The constraint moves. A line we optimized last year is not the same line this year, because the product mix changed, or a new customer pulled differently, or the upstream supplier changed packaging. Flow optimization is not a project you finish. It is a practice you maintain. We help you start it; we do not insist on staying for the rest of its life.
