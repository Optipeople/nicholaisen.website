## The compounding effect

Continuous optimization is not glamorous work. The individual improvements are usually small. A two-minute changeover gain on a beam saw. A scrap reduction from 2.4 percent to 1.9 percent on a particular product family. A handover delay between cells reduced from eight minutes to three.

The reason it matters is compounding. Forty small improvements a year, sustained, lift a line by twenty to thirty percent over thirty-six months. We have watched it happen. The clients who stay with the cadence outperform the clients who chase the next big project.

## What kills the cadence

Two things kill the cadence. The first is the heroic improvement project that absorbs all attention for six weeks. The second is the senior leader who skips the weekly meeting because something more urgent came up. We design the program to survive both, but the design only works if the leadership team commits to it visibly.
