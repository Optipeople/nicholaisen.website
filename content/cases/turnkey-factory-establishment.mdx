## A project with one clear constraint

The contractual shipment date was fixed. Everything else flexed around it. We agreed early with the owner team that we would surface scope or cost trade-offs immediately whenever the schedule was at risk, and they would decide quickly. That discipline held throughout the project and is, in our experience, the single most reliable predictor of an on-time outcome.

<Callout title="The trade-off the owner agreed in month four" tone="accent">
A delay in the kiln vendor's delivery would have pushed first shipment by six weeks. The owner agreed within four working days to specify a smaller secondary kiln from a different vendor as an interim measure, ensuring the schedule held. The interim kiln has since been redeployed to a bottleneck operation; the owner considers the decision one of the best of the project.
</Callout>

## How the facility is laid out

The site is organized around the drying cluster, which is the longest-cycle operation and the one that constrains everything downstream. Primary breakdown sits adjacent to the dry kilns, with intermediate buffering sized to absorb the kiln cycle variance. Finishing operations run on a separate flow with their own buffering, allowing the primary line to run without being held by finishing changeovers. The layout consumes approximately ten percent more floor area than a conventional in-line layout would have done. The flow benefit, particularly during ramp-up, justified the choice many times over.

## What worked

The combination of a single accountable contract, a fixed contractual end date, and a disciplined trade-off process between us and the owner team is what delivered this project on time. Each of those elements would have been weakened by the others' absence. The owner now operates the plant with a small in-house team and a quarterly partnership relationship with us; the project has scaled into a long-running collaboration on capacity planning and continuous improvement.
