## Context

The producer runs two shifts on a single nesting-and-edgebanding line, supplying kitchen carcasses to a network of retail showrooms across Scandinavia. The order book had grown 22 percent year on year. Lead times had moved from 18 to 27 working days, and the planning team was warning of 35-day quotes by the end of the quarter.

The natural conclusion was that capacity was the problem. It usually is, in this segment. The investment case for a second line, on paper, looked solid: payback under four years, defensible against a moderate demand-shrink scenario.

<Callout title="Why the question got asked" tone="info">
The CFO had recently sat through a presentation on capital discipline and asked, more or less casually, whether the existing line was running at the throughput the original specification had promised. The answer turned out to be no. That single question saved the business roughly EUR four million in deferred capital.
</Callout>

## What we found

The line had been specified for a planned OEE of 78 percent and was running at an availability-weighted 54. The gap was not in any single dramatic event. It was distributed across four small ones. Edgebander changeovers averaged 22 minutes against a planned 9. The case-clamping handover queued an average of 14 minutes of WIP that took capacity off the upstream nesting cell. Quality rework absorbed 6 percent of the available time on the edgebander. And the line was being shut down for cleaning thirty minutes early on the second shift, every day, by long-standing convention nobody could explain.

## What we changed

A SMED workshop on the edgebander brought changeovers from 22 minutes to 7 over four weeks of structured work with the line operators. A flow rework on the case-clamping handover, including a small layout change and a rebuilt pull signal, removed roughly twelve minutes of queued WIP per cycle. The cleaning convention was renegotiated and standardized; the line now stops at the planned time and produces six additional units per shift as a result.

The combined effect over the following quarter was a 31 percent throughput lift, an OEE move from 54 to 72, and a lead-time reduction from 27 days to 19.

<PullQuote attribution="Plant manager">
We thought we needed a second line. It turns out we needed a second look at the first one.
</PullQuote>

## What is next

The leadership team has not cancelled the second-line investment. They have paused it. The capacity model now refreshes quarterly against actual throughput and the live order book, and the next investment trigger is set against a forward-looking utilization threshold rather than a feeling. When the model says the second line is needed, it will be ordered. Not before.
