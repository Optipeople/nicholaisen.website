## A consolidation that had to work

Multi-site consolidation projects in this industry have a poor track record. The new facility is invariably more capable than the sum of the old ones, and is invariably twelve to twenty-four months late and over budget. The board knew this. So did the operations leadership.

We took the contract on the explicit basis that the consolidation timeline was non-negotiable. The legacy sites were committed to wind down on a schedule that depended on the new facility producing at planned cadence. Slippage on our side would force the group to operate two sets of fixed costs for longer, eroding the business case rapidly.

<Callout title="The single non-negotiable" tone="accent">
The contract had one hard constraint: the new facility had to absorb the production volume of the first legacy site closure on a fixed date in month seventeen. Every other decision in the project flowed from defending that date.
</Callout>

## How the line is built

The facility runs three integrated lines: a nesting and edgebanding line for the largest product family, a six-sided drilling cell for series production of standard sizes, and a smaller flexible cell for the long tail of customized orders. Material handling is fully automated between primary operations. Operators are deployed at the stations where their judgment and dexterity matter most: quality verification, fault response, and the smaller cell that runs the customized work.

Opti is integrated to every machine, the WMS and the ERP. Every stop is classified at the source, every reject is traced to its decision point, and the daily production report writes itself. Operations leadership compares performance across shifts, lines and product families on a common definition for the first time in the group's history.

## What we learned

Two lessons from this project are worth recording. First, the early decision to instrument from the first commissioning trial paid back many times over. Operators learned the platform during ramp-up, when expectations were forgiving, rather than after handover when every screen is being judged. Second, the single non-negotiable date in the contract was the most useful tool we had. Every scope discussion was framed against it, and decisions that would otherwise have lingered were made cleanly because the date demanded it.

<PullQuote attribution="Group COO">
We have done two consolidations before and never delivered one on schedule. This one was eight weeks early. The difference was the discipline of working back from a single fixed date.
</PullQuote>
